Cells need energy, which photosynthesis and cellular respiration supply in complementary ways. Photosynthesis, occurring in chloroplasts, converts sunlight, water and carbon dioxide into glucose and oxygen through light-dependent reactions, which make ATP and NADPH, and the Calvin cycle, where RuBisCO fixes carbon. Cellular respiration breaks glucose down with oxygen into ATP, water and carbon dioxide via glycolysis and the Krebs cycle in the mitochondria, and fermentation yields little ATP when oxygen is absent. Enzymes regulate these pathways and ATP is the cell's energy currency. Membranes control transport by osmosis and active transport. Energy flows one way through ecosystems, with about ten percent passed between trophic levels.
